# Market Ascent + PLG Era

## Part A — Which level are they on?
Rough revenue markers: ~$250K · ~$1M · ~$5M · ~$100M · $1B+. Use symptoms more than revenue.

| Level | Symptoms | Myth they believe | Next move |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Commodity** | Prospects lump them with everyone else (even if the product is unique); compete on price; tiny margins; customers need lots of hand-holding; feels like treading water | "Business is just a slog" | Product-market fit + positioning: make the difference obvious and *experienceable* before any call |
| **Contender** | Differentiated, but growth needs hustle; revenue lumpy; pockets of traction | "We must serve everyone" | Focus + a GTM motion where the product sells itself; make the advantage **hard to copy** (e.g. a free offer the leader can't match without cannibalizing revenue). Speed AND differentiation |
| **Cusp** | Top 3–5 in category; predictable revenue; profitable; competitors copy features within months | "We need a big team" | A business that runs itself: systems over intuition, less dependence on key people, cut what isn't unique. Danger: coasting on "good enough" |
| **Chosen** | The default choice (Calendly, Slack); word of mouth carries marketing | "The best product wins" | Best product + marketing engine for majority share; deepen moats; expand the market. Niches count: be the obvious choice for one segment |
| **Cause** | Stands for something beyond the product | — | Generosity so large it becomes the moat (Canva, Insight Timer): build margins that let you give away more than anyone can match |

Ask also: where do they want to be in 3 years? The gap is the work.

## Part B — Which PLG era is the product in?
Every era exists because the previous one was too slow to value.
- **PLG 1.0 — User experience:** user is a *Builder* who needs expertise; company shortens signup → value with onboarding tours/checklists. Fine in laggard industries (healthcare, government); fragile in fast-moving ones.
- **PLG 2.0 — Output experience:** AI does the work; user becomes an *Editor* who refines output. Value in ~60 seconds; often the homepage *is* the product. Reaches people previously locked out → bigger market. Trap: bolting a chatbot onto a 1.0 workflow — user still does 90% of the work.
- **PLG 3.0 — Agent experience:** an AI agent orchestrates tools for the user and may choose your product itself. Implication: be easy for agents to evaluate, sign up and use (clear docs, APIs, machine-readable pricing).

**Winning Preference test:** on first touch, does a user instantly feel the old way is obsolete? If not, what output would make them feel it?

Category risk check: could a newcomer remove the need for the user's workflow entirely (as AI image generation did to stock photos)? If yes, redesign around output, not flow.

## Output
1. Level + evidence + the myth to drop
2. PLG era + the gap to the next era
3. Next move in 3 concrete actions
4. Which skill to run next (Commodity → `plg-bullseye-strategy`/`plg-irresistible-offer`; Contender → `plg-free-model`, `plg-bowling-alley-onboarding`; Cusp → `plg-growth-process`, `plg-elite-team`)

Source: *Product-Led Growth* (Wes Bush, 2026 ed.), intro + ch. 7. Unofficial summary.
