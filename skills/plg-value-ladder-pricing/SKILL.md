# Value Ladder Pricing

Pricing must tie your revenue to the customer's success: when they win more, you earn more.

## Four traps to check first
1. **Hidden pricing** — no price = "expensive" + slower decisions.
2. **Misaligned incentives** — price doesn't grow with value (flat revenue per user; a huge customer on a small flat plan).
3. **Big jumps** — free → $2,000/month; start small and expand.
4. **Confusing page** — if a visitor can't pick a plan in ~5 seconds, it's broken.

## Phase 1 — Value metric (1–2 max)
The unit of value exchange (contacts, videos, testimonials collected, revenue processed, appointments booked). Test each candidate:
- Simple for the customer to understand?
- Aligned with the core value? (Often the Key Usage Indicator.)
- Scales with usage — fair to charge more when they get more?
In established markets, use the metric buyers already expect (market scan). Spot it in existing plans: the item present in every tier with a different number. Seats often fail the "aligned" test.

## Phase 2 — Pricing matrix (external face of the DEEP levels from `plg-free-model`)
For each of 3 plans:
1. **Name** — plain (Free / Starter / Pro) or by what it enables.
2. **One-liner** — who it's for + what it lets them do.
3. **Included** — value-metric amount first, then ≤5 highlighted features; tooltips for unclear ones; full comparison table below.
4. **CTA** — identical across plans ("Start free"), "Talk to sales" only where truly necessary; middle plan visually highlighted.

## Phase 3 — Find the price
- Research **one plan at a time**, usually the middle one.
- Ask the right people: ideal free users (for the first paid plan) or current paid customers (for the top plan). Segment ideal vs non-ideal — ideal users may value it 10x more.
- Frame it as a **waitlist / early access**, not a "pricing survey".
- Questions: email · country · most valuable use · at what monthly price is it so expensive you wouldn't consider it · so cheap you'd doubt quality · getting expensive but still possible · a bargain · how disappointed if you could no longer use it (very/somewhat/not).
- Few respondents? Ask just two: acceptable price (good value) and expensive price (would think twice).
- Plot cumulative curves; read the range around where "acceptable" and "too expensive" cross. Don't price below the point users see as good value.
If survey data is provided, compute the curves in code.

## Phase 4 — Pricing page
- **Anchor tagline** — compare to a costlier alternative (an employee, an agency, a competitor) before showing prices.
- **Matrix** with prices, monthly/annual toggle.
- **FAQ** — only questions some (not all) visitors ask; questions everyone asks belong in page copy. Offer chat.
- Optional: why choose us · risk reversal on the biggest risk · ≥3 social proofs mapped to top objections.
Revisit pricing regularly; it's one of the strongest levers.

## Output
Value-metric scorecard · pricing matrix (names, one-liners, inclusions, CTAs) · survey draft or analysis · recommended price range · pricing page copy or audit with the 5-second test result.
