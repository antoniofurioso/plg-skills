# PLG Readiness: Cost of Proof + MOAT

Answers *whether*, *when* and *in what flavour* a business should be product-led. Never a yes/no only — always a when and how.

## Inputs
Business and offer · target customer (SMB / mid-market / enterprise) · price or ACV · how buyers buy today · competitors · what a new customer must do before getting value.

## Step 1 — Define the "product"
In product-led terms, the product is whatever proves value before the sale: a trial, a free tool, a free course, free IP plus a consultative session (for services), a sample.
Product-led means **value before payment** — not necessarily self-serve.

## Step 2 — Product-Led Test (two parts)
1. **Would people pay for the free experience if you charged for it?** Yes → it's a Proof Experience. No → it's a lead magnet (gated PDF, teaser), not PLG.
2. **Can you deliver that proof at a cost that makes sense?** Estimate the **Cost of Proof** = cost to let one prospect experience value.
   - Near zero (software) → offer it to everyone.
   - Moderate/high (setup call, custom audit, physical sample) → qualify first, then prove.
   - Astronomical → not now; note what could lower it (AI often does — re-check yearly).

## Step 3 — Place on the spectrum
| Model | Typical ACV | When |
|---|---|---|
| Sales-led | > $50k | complex, high-stakes, many stakeholders, custom solutions |
| Hybrid (product-led sales) | $5k–$50k | several segments; product proves, sales accelerates big accounts |
| Pure PLG | < $5k | simple, low-risk, intuitive |
Rule: before ~$1M revenue, pick sales-led OR product-led first; layer hybrid later.
Sales helps when it **constructs** the solution; it hurts when it processes a transaction the buyer could do, calls too early, or blocks access ("book a demo" with days of wait — that dead time counts in time-to-value).

## Step 4 — MOAT flags (green / yellow / red)
- **M — Market:** SMB or mid-market = green · mid-market with significant enterprise = yellow (plan hybrid) · pure enterprise = red (structural; one red here is enough to pause full PLG).
- **O — Ocean:** red ocean (buyers already understand the category) = green · blue ocean (must educate) = yellow, unless the product teaches by use in minutes. No red.
- **A — Adoption:** count hurdles — product (learn the tool), knowledge (understand concepts), skill (build a capability). Only product = green · product + knowledge or skill = yellow (plan guided onboarding, in-app education, AI doing the work, humans at key moments) · heavy on all three = red (hybrid with real human help). Watch the founder's curse of knowledge: "it's easy" is rarely true for newcomers.
- **T — Touch-to-value:** no-touch = green · low/high-touch before value = yellow with a plan to reduce · team must be involved before any value = red, but fixable (automation, defaults, education). High-touch early can be a deliberate learning phase.
Flags compound: yellow ocean + yellow adoption = users must learn a new category and a new skill at once — slow down.

## Step 5 — Verdict
- All green → go product-led now.
- Some yellow → go, with a named fix per yellow; if yellows stack, fix foundations first.
- Enterprise-only → use product-led elements (sandbox, proof session, land-and-expand) to support sales.

## Output
1. What the "product" / Proof Experience is (or should be)
2. Product-Led Test result + Cost of Proof estimate
3. Spectrum position
4. MOAT table: flag + one-line reason + fix
5. Verdict and the next skill (usually `plg-preflight-check` or `plg-free-model`)
